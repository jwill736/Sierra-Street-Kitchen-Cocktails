Site Menu Brand Audit

Online presence audit · September 2026

The prices moved to date night. The brand stayed at happy hour.

We looked at Sierra St. Kitchen & Cocktails the way a first-time guest does: search, listings, website, menu and social. Below are ten places it misses the mark, with the evidence for each and what we’d do about it.

03

Ten places it’s missing the mark

Ordered by how much each one costs the restaurant. Evidence is labelled Verified (seen directly), Inferred (strong inference from what we saw) or To verify (needs an owner or in-person check).

01

Priced for date night, listed as a cheap bar

High impactQuick fix~$0

Evidence

  • Site title: Best Bar in Reno. Verified
  • OpenTable: $30 and under and Casual Dining, while entrées run $32–34. Verified
  • Yelp: $$, with Cocktail Bars as the first category. Verified
  • OpenTable Value 3.4, the lowest sub-score. Verified · 2025 snapshot

Listings set expectations before anyone sees the room. A guest who expected a bar and gets a date-night check marks down value, even when the food is good.

Fix: Reposition every listing. Make the primary category New American / small plates, update the price band, and rewrite descriptions around the cellar and the river. The site title becomes the positioning line.

02

A template website that could belong to anyone

High impactProject

Evidence

  • About page, in full: Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet. Verified
  • Homepage headline Well-Planned Evenings Start Here appears word for word on the sites of a sports grill in Indianapolis, a taco shop in Roswell and an Italian restaurant in Livermore. Verified
  • Events page auto-filled with national days. National Taco Day (no tacos on the menu) and National Chocolate Day are listed from 11 AM, for a restaurant that opens at 4. Verified
  • Delivery ordering and job openings are promoted on the homepage; the restaurant’s story isn’t. Verified

Generic copy makes a restaurant feel interchangeable, and interchangeable doesn’t support a premium check.

Fix: A custom site (see the concept) that keeps the reservation and ordering tools they already pay for. Turn off the auto-generated events and write real copy.

03

Five different answers to “are you open?”

High impactQuick fix$0

Evidence

  • Website: Fri–Sat 4 PM–midnight, other days 4–9. Google and Yelp: 4–9 daily. OpenTable: Fri–Sun from noon. MenuPix: opens 4:30, Saturday from noon, Sunday from 3. Verified
  • OpenTable 1★ review: a guest arrived with a reservation to find the doors locked. That was Feb 2025, before the ownership change, but it’s still on the listing. Verified

Every mismatch means an empty seat or a guest at a locked door, and either one can turn into a one-star review.

Fix: Make the Google Business Profile the single source of truth and sync it to Yelp, OpenTable and Apple Maps. The new site reads its live “open now” status from the same hours.

04

One restaurant, six names, two Facebook pages

Medium impactQuick fix

Evidence

  • Sierra Street Kitchen & Cocktails (site), Sierra St Kitchen & Cocktails (Yelp), Sierra St. Kitchen & Cocktails (OpenTable), Sierra Street Kitchen & Cocktail (Facebook), Sierra St. Kitchen & Cocktail (Tripadvisor), Sierra Street Kitchen (online ordering). Verified
  • The active Facebook page shows 0 reviews, while aggregators still show an older Facebook presence at 4.5★ from 34 reviews. Inferred
  • A free Wix site (colbyjayjohnson.wixsite.com/sskc), showing Wix ads, is still indexed by search engines. Verified

Different names split the reviews, the search ranking and the word of mouth. A free site under a personal account looks abandoned.

Fix: Use one name, “Sierra St. Kitchen & Cocktails,” on every listing. Merge or retire the extra Facebook page and unpublish the Wix site.

05

The owners, the chef and the story are invisible

High impactQuick fix

Evidence

  • Longtime manager Colby Johnson and partner Megan Holland took over in April 2025. Neither is named anywhere on the site. Verified
  • OpenTable still lists the previous owner as executive chef. Verified
  • Guests name Chef Chris and servers Greg, Martin and Brendan in their reviews. The site names no one. Verified
  • Several reviews complain about defensive owner replies to criticism (at least one, from 2021, names the previous owner). Verified

“Longtime manager buys the restaurant” is the most persuasive story they have. Telling it also draws a clear line under reviews from the previous ownership.

Fix: A real About page, a named chef, staff portraits, and a review-response playbook: thank them, own it, invite them back.

06

A menu that reads like a draft

High impactQuick fix

Evidence

  • In our crawl of the live menu page, about half the dishes had no price, and one (Tender filet tips served over pillowy gnocchi…) had no name. To verify in a browser
  • Typos: Brussel, Steak Dianne, artisian, paired with with, and both scallopini and Scaloppini. Verified
  • No gluten-free or vegetarian marks, even though the sister restaurant marks the same dishes GF. Verified

At $32–34 an entrée, sloppiness reads as a price-to-quality problem, which is exactly what the Value score picks up.

Fix: Name, spell, price and mark every dish, and publish the same menu on the site, Google and OpenTable.

07

Their best proof of price is buried: the wine list

Medium impactQuick fix

Evidence

  • 35 labels, including Silver Oak, Duckhorn, Jayson by Pahlmeyer and a Renato Ratti Barolo. A wine-list critic called it Downtown Reno’s Most Surprisingly Grown-Up Wine List. Verified
  • On the site it sits below the canned beer with no prices, and with errors: Vueve Clicquot, Barnard Griffan / Bernard Griffon (listed as Oregon; the winery is in Washington), Cio Cairo, Pasó Robles. Verified
  • The same critic rated the glassware Basic Stemmed, and a guest complained about plastic ones you’d find in a cafeteria (on a Valentine’s Day visit). Verified Current glassware: To verify

A serious cellar is what makes a $34 entrée feel fair, but only if guests can see it and the glass matches the bottle.

Fix: Put the cellar up front on the site and publish bottle prices. Upgrade the glassware for signatures and bottles, and launch Cellar Night on the slowest weeknight.

08

A cocktail bar with no visual presence

High impactProject

Evidence

  • No Instagram account found, in search or on the Facebook page. Inferred (ask the owners)
  • The cocktail list gives names and ingredients only: no prices, no descriptions, no story. Verified How much of the site’s imagery is their own: To verify
  • Facebook posts read as auto-generated: Planning a bridal shower, office function, or family reunion in Reno? … #PartyInReno #BookYourEvent. Verified
  • Purple Rain is made with butterfly pea flower, which changes color when citrus hits it. That’s a video moment built into the drink. Verified (the tableside ritual is our proposal)

Date-night guests choose from photos. Without them, nothing online justifies the price.

Fix: A half-day photo and video shoot, an Instagram account led by the tableside Purple Rain, and social captions written by a person.

09

The location’s best moments go unsold

Medium impactProject

Evidence

  • It sits on the Truckee Riverwalk, a short walk from the Pioneer Center, and reviewers mention stopping in before a movie. Verified
  • There’s no pre-show menu, no named patio program and no mention of the Riverwalk. Verified
  • A June 2026 review: I made an early reservation on a Wednesday evening and was the only person here. Verified

Empty 5:00 seatings and quiet Wednesdays are the cheapest revenue to capture, because the kitchen and staff are already on the clock.

Fix: Launch Curtain Call (a pre-show prix fixe: seated by 5:30, out by 7:15) and Cellar Night on Wednesdays, run Golden Hour as a ritual, and mark ten years on Sierra Street.

10

Friction at the check, and a flagship sharing the suburban menu

High impactPolicy

Evidence

  • Reviews cite an automatic 20% service charge followed by a tip line, even for a party of two (2023–2025). Verified Current policy: To verify
  • Holiday menus priced $20–30 above regular items (2023). A 2025 review says tables were so close together, I was touching the lady behind me. Verified
  • Since the takeover, Sierra St. and Skyline Kitchen & Vine share their small bites and big plates, while Skyline runs 50%-off drinks and a Kobe Slider Family Pack. Verified

Surprise charges and cramped tables make a big check feel bigger. And if the downtown flagship serves the suburban sister’s menu, guests have no reason to pay more for it.

Fix: State the service-charge policy up front on the site, the menu and the booking confirmation, and drop the second tip line. Give Sierra St. two or three dishes that only exist downtown. Skyline stays the neighborhood spot; Sierra St. is the night out.

04

What this rebrand is, and isn’t.

A facelift, not a new restaurant. Guests who love Sierra St. today should walk in after the refresh and feel the place has finally caught up with itself.

It is

  • One name, everywhere
  • A house voice in place of template copy
  • A look that matches the check, from the Google listing to the glassware
  • A new site built on the booking and ordering tools they already use
  • Two programs to fill empty seats: Curtain Call and Cellar Night

It isn’t

  • A new name or concept
  • A menu overhaul (the dates stay)
  • A renovation
  • A new reservation system
  • More discounts
05

How we’d roll it out

Cheapest fixes first. Phase one costs almost nothing and removes the biggest trust problems.

Weeks 1–2

Stop the leaks

  • Hours, name and price band fixed on every listing
  • OpenTable profile rewritten, with the right chef listed
  • About page written; Wix site unpublished
Weeks 3–6

Site & menus

  • New website (see concept)
  • Menu and cellar list corrected and priced
  • Events packages and enquiry form
Weeks 6–10

The room

  • Glassware for signatures and bottles
  • Printed menus, coasters, check card
  • Front-window vinyl
Ongoing

Tell it

  • Photo and video half-day
  • Instagram and the River Letter
  • Curtain Call and Cellar Night
